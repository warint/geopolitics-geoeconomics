## 1. The reading — 80 min

**Rose, A. K. (2007). The foreign service and foreign trade: Embassies as export promotion. *The
World Economy*, 30(1), 22–38.** [doi:10.1111/j.1467-9701.2007.00870.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9701.2007.00870.x)
— read in full; a free working-paper version (NBER) circulates under the same title. **Its central
relationship — exports against foreign posts — is today's target.**

**Putnam, R. D. (1988). Diplomacy and domestic politics: The logic of two-level games. *International
Organization*, 42(3), 427–460.** [doi:10.1017/S0020818300027697](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818300027697)
— read the first half carefully: Level I, Level II, the **win-set**.

**Bjola, C., & Manor, I. (2018). Revisiting Putnam's two-level game theory in the digital age:
Domestic digital diplomacy and the Iran nuclear deal. *Cambridge Review of International Affairs*,
31(1), 3–32.** [doi:10.1080/09557571.2018.1476836](https://doi.org/10.1080/09557571.2018.1476836)

Annotate four things:

| | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1** | Rose's **claim**, his **data** (which exporters, which posts, which years) and the **size** of the effect he reports |
| **2** | The **reverse-causality** objection to Rose — and how he answers it |
| **3** | Putnam's **win-set**: what enlarges it, what shrinks it |
| **4** | What Bjola & Manor say **digital** tools change at Level II |

## 3. Self-check — 15 min

1. In Putnam's terms, why can a negotiator with a **small** win-set sometimes obtain a better deal?
2. Rose finds that countries export more to partners where they keep more posts. Give **two**
   explanations other than "embassies promote exports".
3. Name one instrument of **commercial diplomacy** other than an embassy, and one firm that has used it.
4. Investment screening (CFIUS in the US, the EU screening regulation, the *Investment Canada Act*
   national-security review) is economic diplomacy aimed **at firms**. Name one case you know.
