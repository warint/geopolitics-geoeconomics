# Session 09 — Teaching plan (first half, 90 min including Risk Briefs)

# The redefinition of economic diplomacy

> **Instructor page.** The student-facing companion is [`README.md`](README.md); the deck is
> [`INTE60019A-S09-Lecture.qmd`](INTE60019A-S09-Lecture.qmd).

*When states compete for markets, investment and technology, is the multinational the object of
diplomacy, its instrument — or a diplomat in its own right?*

---

## Opening

Before the briefs, three words on the board: **object · instrument · diplomat**. Ask each student to
place one firm they know under one of them. Collect four answers; return to them at minute 75.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **0–20** | **Four Risk Briefs.** |
| **20–30** | **What economic diplomacy has become.** Three shifts. Ask for one example of securitisation affecting a Canadian firm. |
| **30–45** | **Putnam.** Draw two negotiators at Level I and two domestic circles at Level II; shade the overlap of win-sets. Ask: *why is "my parliament will never accept that" a weapon?* Then: where do multinationals sit on this drawing? (In several Level II circles at once.) |
| **45–53** | **Bjola and Manor.** The Iran deal as a digital two-level game. Ask what a firm planning around a negotiated outcome should learn from it. |
| **53–68** | **Rose.** The claim, the data, the gravity logic in one sentence. Then the three objections — write them on the board; they become the "break" step of the practice. Ask the room to propose a test of reverse causality with the data they downloaded. |
| **68–85** | **States and firms.** Stopford & Strange's triangular diplomacy; Vernon's obsolescing bargain. Screening regimes. The commercial-diplomacy toolbox. Return to *object · instrument · diplomat*: did anyone change their mind? |
| **85–90** | **Tableau.** Log axis and trend model on the projector, with the warning about causation. Hand over. |

---

## Run Tableau live

Plot exports against posts on linear axes first — one cloud squashed against the origin, a few giants.
Then switch both axes to logarithmic. Then point out that **zeros cannot be logged**: the partners
with no post fall off the chart. That is the trap of the practice round.

---

## Put this to the room

*"Your firm is negotiating a mining concession abroad and your ambassador offers to raise it with the
president. Under what conditions do you say no?"*

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That a trend line with a high R² and a tiny p-value shows that embassies *cause* exports. It shows a
strong association in a cross-section. Say it at section 4 and again before the trend model goes up.
