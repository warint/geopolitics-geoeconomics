# Session 11 — Weaponised interdependence

### Supply chains and critical minerals

> **When a handful of countries mine and refine what your products cannot be made without, who
> really holds the risk — and what can a multinational do about it?**

## What session 11 is for

| Geopolitics | Data & Tableau | International business |
|---|---|---|
| Explain **weaponised interdependence**: why network hubs become *chokepoints* and *panopticons* | Collect a policy inventory and a production table that do not share a format, and make them share a key | Map a product's bill of materials to the countries that control each input |
| Distinguish **conflict commodities**, **resource nationalism** and **strategic export controls** | Compute a **Herfindahl–Hirschman index** with an LOD expression, and read it against antitrust thresholds | Say what a rising count of export restrictions does — and does not — mean for one supplier contract |
| Place **friend-shoring**, **de-risking** and the EU Critical Raw Materials Act in the logic of the balance of power | Build a treemap and wire a **dashboard action** so a click on one mineral filters the other views | Choose between stockpiling, diversifying, substituting and vertically integrating — and say what each costs |
