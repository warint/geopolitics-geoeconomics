# Weaponised interdependence: supply chains and critical minerals

*When a handful of countries mine and refine what your products cannot be made without, who really
holds the risk?*

---

## Opening — the Risk Briefs (0–20)

Four briefs, four minutes each plus one question. Keep time strictly: stop at five minutes.
After the last brief, ask the room which of the four touched a **supply chain** — usually at least
one did. Use it as the bridge into the lecture.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **20–28** | **From efficiency to exposure.** Ask: *"What is in a smartphone, and where was it refined?"* Collect guesses. Put the minerals of the transition on the board. Link back to Session 02 — the order assumed trade would stay apolitical. |
| **28–42** | **Three ways commodities become geopolitical.** Build the table: conflict commodities, resource nationalism, strategic export controls. Make the room place cobalt in all three rows. Le Billon & Shykora: the supply chain as a *site*, not a pipe; due diligence and its limits — avoidance rather than improvement. |
| **42–52** | **Weaponised interdependence.** Hub-and-spoke on the board. Chokepoint and panopticon. Then the move: *the refinery is the hub*. The two conditions — concentration and domestic institutions. |
| **52–62** | **Export restrictions.** The OECD inventory and its headline. Then insist on the limit: a ban and a licence each count once. Ask: *what would you need to know before telling a buyer that supply is at risk?* |
| **62–78** | **HHI.** Derive it on the board with the self-check example (70/20/10 → 5,400). Ten equal producers → 1,000. Thresholds 2,500 / 1,800. The numbers equivalent. Then the three decisions: stage, "Other countries", units. Make someone compute the HHI with "Other countries" treated as one country, and then dropped. |
| **78–84** | **GSCPI.** What it is, what it is not, the vintage trap. |
| **84–90** | **What the firm can do.** The six strategies; ask each team which two its firm would use. CRMA benchmarks in one sentence. Hand over to the practice — and remind them it is the last practice round before Session 12. |

## Run Tableau live

Open your own transcription and type the share calculation in front of them. Show the wrong version
first — `SUM([Production]) / TOTAL(SUM([Production]))` with the table calculation computed the wrong
way — and watch shares that sum to 400% across four minerals. Then fix it with the FIXED LOD.

---

## Put this to the room

*"Your firm's only qualified supplier of a magnet alloy is in a country that has just introduced an
export licence on the rare earths it uses. The licence has, so far, always been granted. What do you
do this quarter?"*

Push for the distinction between a **measure** and **its enforcement**. A licence that is always
granted is a panopticon before it is a chokepoint — the state now sees every shipment.

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That concentration of **mining** is the risk. The chokepoint is usually at **refining and
processing**, which the USGS mine-production tables do not show. Say it when you introduce the HHI,
and make it the model catch for the practice.
