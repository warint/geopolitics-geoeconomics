# Session 07 — Teaching plan (first half, 90 min)

# Measuring risk: content analysis, coding, big data and composite indices

> **Instructor page.** The student-facing companion is [`README.md`](README.md); the deck is
> [`INTE60019A-S07-Lecture.qmd`](INTE60019A-S07-Lecture.qmd).

*Every geopolitical risk score a multinational buys or builds is a chain of choices. Can you see the
chain — and defend each link?*

This is the methodological hinge of the course and the lecture is short — fifty minutes. Keep it
moving: the practice does the teaching.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **0–20** | **Quiz 2** (Sessions 04–06). Collect at 20 minutes exactly. |
| **20–40** | **Four Risk Briefs.** In each question period, ask one measurement question: *where does that number come from, and who coded it?* |
| **40–47** | **The measurement chain.** Draw it across the board, with the GPR index underneath each link. Validity vs reliability — the thermometer in the wrong room. |
| **47–55** | **Content analysis.** Krippendorff's steps as a table, GPR in one column, their own coding in the other. Manifest vs latent: dictionaries are perfectly reliable and blind. |
| **55–65** | **Three dictionaries.** GPR, EPU, PRisk side by side. The key move in Hassan et al.: the dictionary is *learned* from political vs non-political texts. The key finding: most political risk is firm-specific. Ask: *what does that mean for a firm that buys a country-risk score?* |
| **65–75** | **Reliability.** Kappa on the board; do the worked example together — make them compute $p_e$. Ask a pair to say their own percent agreement out loud, then predict whether their kappa will be higher or lower. |
| **75–80** | **Big data.** Four traps in four sentences. Detection vs measurement. |
| **80–90** | **Composite indicators.** The five decisive steps. Min–max on the board, with the direction flip. Compensability in one example: a country with excellent governance and an active war averages to "medium". Hand over: *your composite today is your team's draft.* |

## Run Tableau live

Skip it at the lecture's end — there is no time — and instead start the practice by building the
coder A × coder B highlight table live on one team's data (ask for a volunteer in advance). Turning
on *Analysis → Totals → Show Row/Column Grand Totals* gives every number in the kappa formula.

---

## Put this to the room

*"A consultancy sells your board a country-risk score from 0 to 100. What five questions do you ask
before you let it into a decision?"*

Push for the chain: what concept; which sources; who or what codes; how normalised and weighted; how
sensitive. If they get all five, they have learned the session.

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That more data means better measurement. GDELT's millions of records are a biased sample measured
with error; forty headlines carefully coded by two people can be more *valid* for a specific
question. Say it in section 5 and again when the practice starts.
