# Session 04 — Teaching plan (first half, 90 min)

# Trade, conflict and geoeconomic fragmentation

> **Instructor page.** The student-facing companion is [`README.md`](README.md); the deck is
> [`INTE60019A-S04-Lecture.qmd`](INTE60019A-S04-Lecture.qmd).

*If states trade more with friends than with rivals, how does a multinational find out which side
each of its markets is on — and how fast the lines are hardening?*

---

## Opening — Quiz 1 (20 min) and Risk Briefs (20 min)

**0–20 · Quiz 1.** Distribute on paper at minute 0, collect at minute 20 sharp. Sessions 01–03:
Part A concepts (4), Part B the big work (3), Part C an unseen chart (3). Have the Part C chart printed
in colour.

**20–40 · Risk Briefs.** Four briefs, five-minute slots (four minutes + one question). The lecture is
compressed to fifty minutes today: keep the briefs strictly to time.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **40–48** | **The liberal answer.** Montesquieu and Angell on the board; then the four mechanisms. Ask: *which mechanism would your firm's lobbyists embody?* |
| **48–56** | **The realist critique.** Vulnerability, relative gains, Gowa's security externalities, Copeland's expectations. 1914 as the cautionary case. |
| **56–62** | **Mansfield's verdict.** The four points, briskly. Emphasise reverse causality — it returns in every practice round. |
| **62–70** | **Martin, Mayer & Thoenig.** The two-row table. Ask the room to predict the sign of multilateral openness before revealing it. The uncomfortable implication: globalisation lowers the cost of bilateral conflict. |
| **70–82** | **Fragmentation.** Friend-shoring, de-risking, de-coupling — three words, three policies. Gopinath et al.'s three steps. Ask who wrote down the bloc definition; have them read it. Then: *how will ours differ?* |
| **82–90** | **The firm, and what the figure does not license.** One implication only — classify your markets — and the four limits. Open Tableau: show the data model with the bloc table added twice. Hand over. |

## Run Tableau live

The double relationship is the hard part of the round. Show it on the projector: drag the bloc table
in, relate on exporter; drag it in again — Tableau names the second copy `(1)` — relate on partner;
rename the second copy "Partner blocs". Then show a `Pair type` field returning "Within" and
"Between". Five minutes here saves thirty in the practice.

---

## Put this to the room

*"Your firm sells industrial equipment. Three of its five main markets are non-aligned. Is that a risk
or an opportunity?"*

Push for both: opportunity as connectors, risk as targets of secondary sanctions and transshipment
enforcement.

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That the fall in between-bloc trade **proves** geopolitics drove it. Prices, sanctions on Russia and
the post-pandemic cycle all move in 2022. Say it when the finding appears and again in the limits.
