## 2. The reading — 60–75 min

**Gopinath, G., Gourinchas, P.-O., Presbitero, A. F., & Topalova, P. (2025). Changing global
linkages: A new Cold War? *Journal of International Economics*, 153, 104042.**
[doi:10.1016/j.jinteco.2024.104042](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinteco.2024.104042) · free as IMF
Working Paper WP/24/76.

**Mansfield, E. D. (2021). International trade and conflict.** In J. C. W. Pevehouse & L. Seabrooke
(Eds.), *The Oxford handbook of international political economy* (pp. 660–671). Oxford University
Press. [doi:10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198793519.013.27](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198793519.013.27)

Read Mansfield first — it is short and gives you the debate. Then Gopinath et al.: introduction, the
section that **defines the blocs**, and the figures on trade and investment between blocs.

Annotate four things:

| | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1** | Mansfield: the **liberal** argument that trade reduces conflict, the **realist** reply, and his own verdict |
| **2** | Gopinath et al.: **exactly how blocs are defined** — which countries, which vote or measure, which year |
| **3** | The **target figure**: trade within vs between blocs, before and after the 2022 invasion. Its base period, its frequency, its units |
| **4** | The role of **connector** countries — and one your team's firm depends on |

---

## 3. The data — 20 min

Download the ideal-point file and codebook from Harvard Dataverse, and build an IMF IMTS extract of
bilateral goods exports, annual, 2015–latest. Details: [`../data/README.md`](../data/README.md).

The IMF extract is the largest download so far: do it before you read, not in class.

---

## 4. Self-check — 15 min

Answer on paper.

1. State the liberal "commercial peace" argument in one sentence, and the realist objection in one.
2. Martin, Mayer & Thoenig (2008) distinguish bilateral and multilateral openness. Which one makes a
   bilateral conflict *more* likely, and why?
3. What does a UN General Assembly ideal point measure? Name one thing it does not.
4. Why might trade between two blocs fall even if no government imposes a single new barrier?
