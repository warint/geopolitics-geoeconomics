# Session 06 — Teaching plan (first half, 90 min)

# Private authority, standards and global regulation

> **Instructor page.** The student-facing companion is [`README.md`](README.md); the deck is
> [`INTE60019A-S06-Lecture.qmd`](INTE60019A-S06-Lecture.qmd).

*Who writes the rules a multinational actually follows?*

---

## Opening

Hold up a phone. Ask: *"How many standards are in this object, and how many were written by a
government?"* Let them list: radio (3GPP), Wi-Fi (IEEE), USB-C (USB-IF, then made mandatory by the
EU), the factory's ISO 9001 and 14001 certificates, the conflict-minerals due diligence of its supplier.
Write them on the board; place them in the triangle in section 2.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **0–20** | **Four Risk Briefs.** |
| **20–28** | **The rules a firm follows.** From the phone list to the concept of private authority — risk and lever. |
| **28–42** | **The governance triangle.** Draw it. Place the phone's standards, the FSC, the Global Compact, ISO 14001 — let the room argue the placements; there is not always one right answer. Then the shadow of the state: USB-C is the perfect example — private standard, made mandatory by the EU. |
| **42–55** | **Standards and power.** Mattli and Büthe's three explanations on the board; ask the room to predict which wins before revealing. Then to geopolitics: 5G, batteries, AI; the Brussels effect in one sentence. |
| **55–63** | **Brunsson et al. and Green**, briskly: *of / by / as* organisation; delegated vs entrepreneurial. One example each. The decoupling point: a certificate is not a practice. |
| **63–80** | **Race to the bottom or trading up?** Draw the two scenarios as arrows between an exporter and a strict market. Prakash and Potoski's design and finding. Ask: *what would a race to the bottom look like in the ISO data?* (Adoption falling as exports to lax markets rise.) |
| **80–90** | **The Tableau skill.** Live: geographic role on `iso3`, the unknown-locations indicator, a filled map, `Year` on Pages. Hand over. |

## Run Tableau live

Load a small ISO table with country *names* first and show the "unknown" count — Hong Kong, Taiwan,
Korea will fail or land oddly. Then switch to `iso3`. One minute that saves a dozen broken maps.

---

## Put this to the room

*"Your firm can join the ISO technical committee that will write the next version of a standard in
your industry. It costs two engineers a quarter of their time for three years. Worth it?"*

Push for Mattli and Büthe's answer: if your competitors' national standards body is better organised
than yours, the standard will be theirs — and you will pay to comply with it.

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That "voluntary" means "optional". For an exporter, a standard required by its buyers is no more
optional than a law. Say it early and again when presenting Prakash and Potoski.
