# Session 06 — Pre-session preparation

> **Most of the rules that govern a multinational's daily operations were not written by a
> government. Who wrote them, with whose power — and does trade spread them up or down?**

> **[Open the pre-session slides](https://warint.github.io/geopolitics-geoeconomics/session-06-pre-session.html)** — or read the source at [`INTE60019A-S06-Pre-Session.qmd`](INTE60019A-S06-Pre-Session.qmd).

Three things to do before class. Budget **about two and a half hours**, and **start the ISO Survey
download at least two days ahead**: it needs a free IAF CertSearch account.

| | Before class | Used in class for |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Read the paper; skim the triangle | the lecture and the replication |
| **2** | Collect three datasets | Practice round 06 |
| **3** | Self-check | the first ten minutes of the lecture |

---

## 1. The reading — 75–90 min

**The big work.** Prakash, A., & Potoski, M. (2006). Racing to the bottom? Trade, environmental
governance, and ISO 14001. *American Journal of Political Science*, 50(2), 350–364.
[doi:10.1111/j.1540-5907.2006.00188.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-5907.2006.00188.x)

| | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1** | The **research question**: does trade drive a race to the bottom in environmental governance? |
| **2** | The **mechanism**: why would a country's adoption depend on adoption in its **export markets**? |
| **3** | The **strongest evidence** — what is the panel, and what is the key variable? |
| **4** | One **limitation** — from a referee, or from a firm deciding whether to certify |

**Skim, for the lecture** (30 min in total — the abstract, the introduction and the main figure or
table of each):

- Abbott, K. W., & Snidal, D. (2009). The governance triangle: Regulatory standards institutions and
  the shadow of the state. In W. Mattli & N. Woods (Eds.), *The Politics of Global Regulation*
  (pp. 44–88). Princeton University Press. [doi:10.1515/9781400830732.44](https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400830732.44)
  — **draw the triangle.**
- Mattli, W., & Büthe, T. (2003). Setting international standards: Technological rationality or
  primacy of power? *World Politics*, 56(1), 1–42. [doi:10.1353/wp.2004.0006](https://doi.org/10.1353/wp.2004.0006)
  — **write down the three explanations they compare.**

---

## 2. The data — 45 min (plus the account wait)

Details and the fallback: [`../data/README.md`](../data/README.md). Split the work in your team.

1. **ISO Survey** — ISO 14001 and ISO 9001 certificates by country and year. If you cannot download
   the full tables, use the **transcription fallback** (35 countries × 6 years).
2. **IMF IMTS** — goods exports from your ~35 countries to all partners, same years.
3. **WDI GDP** — current US$, all countries.

Everything goes, untouched, into `06-private-authority-and-standards/data/raw/`.

---

## 3. Self-check — 15 min

1. Name the three corners of Abbott and Snidal's triangle, and place **one** scheme you know (e.g. the
   Forest Stewardship Council, the UN Global Compact, ISO 14001) somewhere inside it.
2. What is a "race to the bottom"? What is a "California effect" (Vogel, 1995)? Which does Prakash and
   Potoski's evidence support?
3. Why would a firm in Viet Nam certify to ISO 14001 if no Vietnamese law requires it?
4. Who sits at the table when an ISO standard is written? Why might that matter for a firm from a
   country that is not there?
