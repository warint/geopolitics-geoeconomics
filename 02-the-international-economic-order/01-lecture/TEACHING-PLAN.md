# Session 02 — Teaching plan (first half, 90 min)

# The international economic order

> **Instructor page.** The student-facing companion is [`README.md`](README.md); the deck is
> [`INTE60019A-S02-Lecture.qmd`](INTE60019A-S02-Lecture.qmd).

*Who wrote the rules multinationals play by — and what happens to the rules when the writers' share
of the world shrinks?*

---

## Opening — Risk Briefs (20 min)

The first four Risk Briefs of the term. Keep them to five minutes each, hard stop at six, and hold
questions to **one per brief** today so the block fits twenty minutes. Set the tone: after the first
brief, ask the room *"Which channel?"* — and accept only one of the five from Session 01.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **20–28** | **What an order is.** The three questions on the board — who wrote the rules, what they say, what holds them. Leave them up for the hour. |
| **28–42** | **Four layers of the post-1945 order.** Bretton Woods, GATT, embedded liberalism, the WTO. Ask: *which of these does your team's firm still rely on daily?* (Most will say the WTO without knowing it.) Ikenberry's "easy to join, hard to overturn". |
| **42–60** | **Baldwin's three unbundlings.** Three costs on the board: goods, ideas, people. Draw the North–South income ratio rising then falling. Insist on the key point: in the second unbundling the multinational is the vehicle of convergence. Show Baldwin's figure and ask what its axis measures. |
| **60–72** | **From convergence to backlash.** Rodrik's trilemma as a triangle. Place Brexit, the 2018 tariffs and export controls on it. Slowbalisation: show the trade/GDP line and ask whether it is retreat or a change of form. |
| **72–82** | **What it means for the firm.** Four implications. Spend time on the Appellate Body: what does a firm do when the dispute mechanism is unavailable? |
| **82–90** | **What the figure does not license**, then open Tableau: connect to a DataBank extract and **pivot** live — the single most useful move in the course. Hand over. |

---

## Run Tableau live

Pivot in front of them, then show that `Value` is now a string because of DataBank's `..`. Change the
type to *Number (decimal)* and show the nulls appear. Say: this is why we look at the grid before we
build anything.

---

## Put this to the room

*"At market exchange rates and at PPP, the G7's share tells two different stories. You are briefing
a board deciding where to build a plant. Which number do you show them, and why?"*

Push for the answer that depends on the decision: **market exchange rates** for where purchasing power
to buy *your* exported product sits; **PPP** for the size of real production and local markets.

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That a falling G7 share means the G7 economies shrank. It is a share: the denominator grew faster.
Say it once when the figure first appears and once at the end.
