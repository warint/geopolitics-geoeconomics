# Session 02 — The international economic order

### The system in which multinational firms operate

> **Who wrote the rules multinationals play by — and what happens to the rules when the writers'
> share of the world shrinks?**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Big work** | Baldwin, R. (2016). *The Great Convergence: Information Technology and the New Globalization*. Belknap Press of Harvard University Press. |
| **Target figure** | The **G7 share of world GDP**, 1970–today — the "great convergence" that begins around 1990 |
| **Data you collect** | World Bank, World Development Indicators: GDP in current US$ (`NY.GDP.MKTP.CD`), GDP at PPP (`NY.GDP.MKTP.PP.CD`), trade in % of GDP (`NE.TRD.GNFS.ZS`) — [DataBank WDI](https://databank.worldbank.org/source/world-development-indicators) |
| **Tableau skill** | **Pivot** wide years to long · filter on ISO codes, not names · area chart · **percent of total** |
| **Course themes** | The system of international economic order in which multinational firms operate |

---

## What session 02 is for

| Geopolitics | Data & Tableau | International business |
|---|---|---|
| Describe the **post-1945 economic order**: Bretton Woods, GATT and the WTO, embedded liberalism | Download a custom extract from World Bank DataBank and log it | Explain why the second unbundling made the multinational the **organiser of global value chains** |
| State Baldwin's **three unbundlings** and why the second produced the great convergence | Pivot sixty year-columns into two: `Year` and `Value` | Say what a shrinking G7 share means for who sets standards, sanctions and market-access rules |
| State Rodrik's **trilemma** and use it to read the backlash against hyper-globalisation | Build the G7 share with a calculated field and with a percent-of-total table calculation | Read "slowbalisation" in trade/GDP and say what it does and does not imply for a firm's footprint |
