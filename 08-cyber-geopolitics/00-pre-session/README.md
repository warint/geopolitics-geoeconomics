## 1. The reading — 75 min

**Valeriano, B., & Maness, R. C. (2015). *Cyber War versus Cyber Realities: Cyber Conflict in the
International System*. Oxford University Press.**
[doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190204792.001.0001](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780190204792.001.0001)
— available through HEC's library (Oxford Academic). Read the introduction, the chapter presenting
their theory of **cyber restraint**, and the chapter describing the incident data and its patterns.

**Farrell, H., & Newman, A. L. (2019). Weaponized interdependence: How global economic networks
shape state coercion. *International Security*, 44(1), 42–79.**
[doi:10.1162/isec_a_00351](https://doi.org/10.1162/isec_a_00351) — read in full; you will use it
again in Sessions 10 and 11.

Annotate four things:

| | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1** | Valeriano & Maness's **claim**: who uses cyber operations against whom, how severe they are, and why |
| **2** | How their **data** are built: which dyads enter, what counts as an incident, how severity is scored |
| **3** | Farrell & Newman's two mechanisms: the **panopticon** effect and the **chokepoint** effect |
| **4** | One reason a multinational with no quarrel with anyone might still be **hit** |

**Today's target figure** is the book's descriptive picture of incidents: their count over time, by
method and by severity, and who targets whom.

## 2. The data — 15 min

Download, untouched, into `08-cyber-geopolitics/data/raw/`. Details: [`../data/README.md`](../data/README.md).

- **DCID v2.0** — Harvard Dataverse [doi:10.7910/DVN/CQOMYV](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/CQOMYV),
  file `Master_DCID_2.0_Release_public_Sep2022.tab`, downloaded **as CSV** (*Access File → Comma
  Separated Values (Original File Format)*). The codebook is not on Dataverse: download *Codebook 2.0*
  from the authors' site, [ryanmaness.com/cyber-conflict-dataset](https://www.ryanmaness.com/cyber-conflict-dataset).
- **EuRepoC v1.3** — Zenodo [doi:10.5281/zenodo.14965395](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14965395),
  file `eurepoc_global_dataset_1_3.csv`, plus the codebook `eurepoc_codebook_1_2.pdf` (same record).

Open the DCID codebook and find: the unit of observation, the list of methods, and the severity scale.

## 4. Self-check — 15 min

1. Valeriano & Maness argue for **restraint**. Restraint by whom, and why would a state hold back a
   weapon it has?
2. What is the difference between Farrell & Newman's **panopticon** and **chokepoint** effects? Give
   one example of each that involves a private firm.
3. Why is **attribution** a problem for anyone building a dataset of state cyber operations?
4. Name one multinational that suffered losses in a cyber operation it was not the target of.
