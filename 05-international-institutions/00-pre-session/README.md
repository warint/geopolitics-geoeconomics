# Session 05 — Pre-session preparation

> **When a multinational relies on an international institution to manage its risk, is it relying on
> a rule — or on the states that buy and sell influence inside it?**

Slides: [`INTE60019A-S05-Pre-Session.qmd`](INTE60019A-S05-Pre-Session.qmd)

Three things to do before class. Budget **about two hours**.

| | Before class | Used in class for |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Read the paper | the lecture discussion and the replication |
| **2** | Collect the data — route A or route B, decided as a team | Practice round 05 |
| **3** | Self-check | the first ten minutes of the lecture |

---

## 1. The reading — 60–75 min

**Kuziemko, I., & Werker, E. (2006). How much is a seat on the Security Council worth? Foreign aid
and bribery at the United Nations. *Journal of Political Economy*, 114(5), 905–930.**
[doi:10.1086/507155](https://doi.org/10.1086/507155)

Read for the **argument**, not for coverage. Annotate four things:

| | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1** | The **research question**, in one sentence |
| **2** | The **identification idea**: why does the rotation of elected seats let the authors separate the effect of the seat from everything else about a country? |
| **3** | The **strongest single piece of evidence** — and the evidence that the effect is larger when the Council matters more |
| **4** | One **limitation** you would raise as a referee — or as a firm relying on a UN resolution |

**The target figure** is the paper's evidence on aid **in event time**: aid in the years before,
during and after a country's term, year by year. Find where the paper shows it — as a figure, and
in the year-by-year estimates behind it — and write down what is on each axis.

**Background, optional:** Keohane, R. O. (1984). *After Hegemony: Cooperation and Discord in the
World Political Economy*. Princeton University Press — chapter 6, on why states build institutions.
Mearsheimer, J. J. (1994/95). The false promise of international institutions. *International
Security*, 19(3), 5–49 — the realist reply.

---

## 2. The data — 30–45 min

Decide as a team which route you take: details in [`../data/README.md`](../data/README.md).

- **Route A.** Download the authors' replication file from AidData. Open it; write the variable list
  and units in your log. The link is a Google Drive file that asked for a sign-in when checked
  (October 2026): if it will not open, switch to route B.
- **Route B.** Download the ForeignAssistance.gov Country Summary, `us_foreign_aid_country.csv`
  (official site or the mirror), and
  **transcribe** the UN list of elected Council members, 1970–2010, one row per term. Split the
  alphabet between team members — it takes each of you about twenty minutes.

Everything goes, untouched, into `05-international-institutions/data/raw/`.

---

## 3. Self-check — 15 min

Answer on paper.

1. In one sentence each: why do states build institutions, according to a **liberal
   institutionalist** and according to a **realist**?
2. How many members does the Security Council have, how many are permanent, and how long is an
   elected member's term?
3. Why does the paper compare a country with **itself** in other years rather than with countries
   that never served?
4. A Security Council resolution under Chapter VII imposes sanctions on a state where your firm has
   a distributor. Who is bound by it — and why should the firm care how the resolution was voted?
