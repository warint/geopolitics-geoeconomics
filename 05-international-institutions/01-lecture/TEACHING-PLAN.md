# Session 05 — Teaching plan (first half, 90 min)

# International institutions: arenas of power, or constraints on it?

> **Instructor page.** The student-facing companion is [`README.md`](README.md); the deck is
> [`INTE60019A-S05-Lecture.qmd`](INTE60019A-S05-Lecture.qmd).

*When a multinational relies on an international institution to manage its risk, is it relying on a
rule — or on the states that buy and sell influence inside it?*

---

## Opening

Before any slide, ask: *"Your firm's lawyers tell you a contract is safe because the host country has
signed an investment treaty with Canada. What exactly are you relying on?"* Collect two answers.
Return to them in section 4.

---

## Board plan

| Minutes | |
|---|---|
| **0–20** | **Four Risk Briefs**, five-minute slots (four minutes + one question). Keep strict time; one question from the room per brief. |
| **20–35** | **Why institutions under anarchy?** Keohane and Mearsheimer side by side; build the four-row table on the board. Ask: *which of the two would explain the WTO Appellate Body's paralysis since 2019?* |
| **35–43** | **The Council in five facts.** Draw the fifteen seats; shade the P5; write "9 votes, no veto". Ask why the P5 need the elected members at all. |
| **43–65** | **The price of a seat.** The question; then the identification idea on the board — *a country compared with itself*. Draw the expected event-time shape before showing the result: flat, up in years 0 and +1, back down. Then the salience result: why it turns a correlation into a mechanism. Ask what alternative explanation is left standing. |
| **65–80** | **What it means for firms.** The four-row table: sanctions, investment protection, insurance, trade rules. Return to the opening answers. Land the lesson: track the coalition, not just the rule. |
| **80–90** | **The Tableau skill.** Open Tableau on the projector; write the `[Event time]` and `[Baseline aid]` calculations live; explain why `FIXED` and not a table calculation. Hand over. |

## Run Tableau live

Join the term table to the aid table on the ISO code (double-click into the logical table to reach the join layer) and show the row count before and after. Then filter
`[Event time]` to −3…+3 and show that a country with two terms close together appears twice in
some years. Ask whether that is a problem. (It is a feature of event studies — overlapping windows —
and it goes in the log.)

---

## Put this to the room

*"If a seat on the Security Council can be bought, should a firm treat a UN sanctions resolution as
more or less durable than a unilateral US one?"*

Push for the mechanism: UN resolutions need a broad coalition and survive changes of government in
any one capital; unilateral measures can be reversed by one executive — but also extended by one.

---

## Misconception to pre-empt

That the paper proves "corruption at the UN". It shows that aid moves with membership and with the
Council's salience; *bribery* is the authors' interpretation of a well-identified pattern. Make the
room separate the finding from the reading of it — it is the same discipline as "the catch".
