# Team work — a geopolitical risk assessment for one multinational (20%)

> **Design the geopolitical risk assessment a real multinational should be running — build it, and
> defend the method behind it to its board.**

Teams of three or four, formed at the start of Session 02. Each team adopts **one named
multinational** for the whole term — no two teams the same firm.

---

## What you deliver

| Deliverable | Points | When |
|---|---|---|
| **The dashboard** — a Tableau Public workbook | part of the 15 below | one week after Session 12 |
| **The memo to the board** — 2,500 words, plus appendices | 15 (dashboard + memo together) | one week after Session 12 |
| **The presentation and defence** — 12 minutes + 8 minutes of questions | 5 | Session 12 |

### The dashboard

At least **four datasets** collected in the practice rounds, from at least **three of the six
families of indicators** (Session 01): event-based, text-based, expert-based, structural,
market-based, firm-level. It must contain:

1. **The firm's footprint** — where its revenue, assets, suppliers and people are (from its own
   disclosures), as a map or a table.
2. **A composite exposure index** for the firm's main countries, built with the method of Session 07:
   indicators chosen and justified, normalised, weighted by **parameters** the board can move.
3. **At least two channel views** — e.g. sanctions exposure (S10), dependence on satellite services (S11),
   bloc alignment of key markets (S04), cyber-incident exposure of its sector (S08).
4. **A watchlist** — three indicators, each with a threshold that triggers a named decision.

### The memo

| Section | Words | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Executive summary | 250 | The three risks that matter, and what to do |
| The firm and its footprint | 300 | Where it is exposed, and through which channels |
| Method | 700 | Which indicators, why these, how combined, what each cannot see — the Session 07 discipline |
| Findings | 800 | What the dashboard shows, risk by risk, interaction by interaction |
| Recommendations and watchlist | 450 | Decisions, triggers, owners |
| Appendices | — | Provenance log for every dataset; data decisions; contribution statement |

## Rubric (20 points)

| Criterion | Points | Full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| **Conceptual grounding** | 3 | The risks are framed with the course's concepts — order, anarchy, institutions, diplomacy, sanctions, interdependence — not newspaper vocabulary |
| **Data and provenance** | 3 | Four or more datasets collected at source, every one logged; decisions named |
| **Method of the composite index** | 4 | Indicators justified; normalisation and weighting explained; sensitivity to weights shown |
| **Analysis of interactions** | 3 | Shows how risks compound or offset — e.g. sanctions *and* input concentration *and* bloc alignment |
| **Recommendations** | 2 | Decisions with triggers and owners, not "monitor the situation" |
| **Presentation and defence** | 5 | Twelve minutes, the dashboard driven live, questions answered by any member |

**Individual accountability.** Each memo ends with a contribution statement signed by all members.
At the end of term each student submits a confidential peer rating (four dimensions, 1–5). The team
mark is multiplied by an individual factor (default 1.00, range 0.80–1.05) only when the peer
ratings and the instructor's observation of the practice rounds independently agree.
