# Quizzes (15% — three × 5%)

Twenty minutes, at the start of Sessions **04, 07 and 10**, on paper, closed book, individual.

| Quiz | Session | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Quiz 1** | 04 | Sessions 01–03: what geopolitical risk is; the international economic order; anarchy and the balance of power |
| **Quiz 2** | 07 | Sessions 04–06: trade, conflict and fragmentation; international institutions; private authority and standards |
| **Quiz 3** | 10 | Sessions 07–09: measuring risk; cyber geopolitics; economic diplomacy |

## The format — 10 points each

| Part | Points | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| **A · Concepts** | 4 | Four short definitions or distinctions, one or two sentences each |
| **B · The big work** | 3 | The research question, the data and the central finding of one of the session readings — and one limitation |
| **C · Read the chart** | 3 | An unseen Tableau chart: say what it shows, what decision it was built with, and what it does not license a firm to conclude |

---

## Specimen — Quiz 1

**A · Concepts (4 points, 1 each)**

1. Distinguish *risk* from *uncertainty* in Knight's sense, and give one geopolitical example of each.
2. What is the difference between Kobrin's *macro* and *micro* political risk?
3. In the GPR index, why are counts of geopolitical articles divided by the total number of articles?
4. What does "anarchy" mean in the realist tradition — and what does it *not* mean?

**B · The big work (3 points)**

Caldara and Iacoviello (2022) distinguish *GPR Threats* from *GPR Acts*. (a) Which categories enter
each? (b) Why does the distinction matter for a firm's investment decision? (c) Name one limitation
of the index for a firm whose operations are mainly in sub-Saharan Africa.

**C · Read the chart (3 points)**

*[A stacked area chart: shares of world GDP, current US dollars, 1970–2023, for the G7, China, and
the rest of the world.]*

(a) State the finding in one sentence. (b) The chart uses current US dollars. How would it change at
purchasing-power parity, and why? (c) What does the chart *not* tell a Canadian manufacturer deciding
where to build its next plant?

---

## Specimen answers (Part A)

1. *Risk*: probabilities estimable from experience (piracy losses on a shipping route). *Uncertainty*:
   probabilities not estimable (whether a given strait will be blockaded within five years).
2. *Macro* risk affects all foreign firms in a country (a general expropriation, currency
   inconvertibility); *micro* risk affects one sector or firm (a mining-code change, a contract
   cancellation).
3. Because newspapers' total output changes over time; the share removes the effect of size, so the
   index measures *relative* attention to geopolitical tension.
4. The absence of a government above states — no authority to enforce agreements. It does not mean
   chaos or constant war.
