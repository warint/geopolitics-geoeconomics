12.0.1 A geopolitical risk assessment for one multinational

Can you make a board act on your assessment — and say what would change your mind?

What happens Each team presents and defends its geopolitical risk dashboard for its multinational
Assessment Presentation and defence: 5 of the 20 points of the team work. Memo and published dashboard (15 points) due one week later
Closing readings Kobrin, S. J. (1979). Political risk: A review and reconsideration. Journal of International Business Studies, 10(1), 67–80. doi:10.1057/palgrave.jibs.8490631 · Henisz, W. J. (2000). The institutional environment for economic growth. Economics & Politics, 12(1), 1–31. doi:10.1111/1468-0343.00066
Deck INTE60019A-S12-Presentations.qmd
Brief assessments/team-project/

There is no pre-session reading beyond your own work, no practice round and no Risk Brief.

Lecture notes: this session is also Chapter 12 of the course book.

12.1 Run of show

Each team has 20 minutes: 12 minutes of presentation, the dashboard driven live, then 8 minutes of defence — questions from the instructor and from the room.

Time
0:00–0:05 Order of presentation drawn at random; the two-team “panel” for each slot announced
0:05–2:50 Teams 1–8, 20 minutes each, back to back. A five-minute break after team 4
2:50–3:00 Closing address: from indicators to decisions

With more than eight teams the session runs in two rooms in parallel, or is extended by agreement; the order and room are posted on ZoneCours the week before.

The panel. For each presentation, two other teams act as the board. Each prepares one question in advance from the published dashboard link (sent 48 hours before) and asks it in the defence. The quality of a team’s board question counts towards its members’ participation.

Who answers. The instructor addresses at least one question to a member drawn at random. Every member must be able to defend every part of the dashboard and the method behind it.

12.2 What the twelve minutes must contain

Minutes What the board must see
0–2 The firm and its footprint: where its revenue, assets, suppliers and people are
2–5 The composite exposure index for its main countries — indicators, normalisation, weights — and one live move of a weight parameter to show what the ranking is sensitive to
5–9 Two channel views, driven live: e.g. sanctions, critical inputs, bloc alignment, cyber
9–11 The interaction: how two risks compound or offset for this firm
11–12 Decisions and the watchlist: three indicators, three thresholds, three named decisions

Overrun is stopped at thirteen minutes.

12.3 Rubric (20 points — consistent with the team-work brief)

Criterion Points Assessed Full marks look like
Conceptual grounding 3 memo + presentation Risks framed with the course’s concepts — order, anarchy, institutions, diplomacy, sanctions, interdependence — not newspaper vocabulary
Data and provenance 3 memo Four or more datasets collected at source, from at least three families of indicators, every one logged; decisions named
Method of the composite index 4 memo Indicators justified; normalisation and weighting explained; sensitivity to weights shown
Analysis of interactions 3 memo How risks compound or offset — e.g. sanctions and input concentration and bloc alignment
Recommendations 2 memo Decisions with triggers and owners, not “monitor the situation”
Presentation and defence 5 today Twelve minutes; the dashboard driven live; questions answered by any member, including the one drawn at random

Presentation and defence (5 points), in detail:

Points
2 Clarity and time: the five blocks above, in twelve minutes, the dashboard driven rather than screenshotted
2 Defence: answers that engage the question, concede what the data cannot show, and say what would change the recommendation
1 Random-member answer: the member drawn answers competently without help

Individual accountability (the peer-rating multiplier) is described in the team-work brief.

12.4 The closing address — from indicators to decisions

Ten minutes, after the last team. Its content is in the deck; in summary:

  1. Kobrin (1979) was right about the hard part. Political risk is not the event — it is the contingency that the event affects this firm’s operations. Every dashboard presented today is a claim about that contingency.
  2. Henisz (2000) on constraint. The POLCON index measures how many independent veto players constrain a government. Low constraint means policy can change fast — in either direction. It is the structural complement to every event- and text-based indicator of the course.
  3. Indicators do not decide. A watchlist is useful only if each indicator has a threshold and an owner. The best dashboards today were the ones that would make someone do something on a given Monday.
  4. What the twelve weeks established. Geopolitical risk is measured through families of indicators that disagree; each figure in a famous work is a stack of decisions; and the analyst’s job is to make those decisions visible to the person who must act.

12.5 After today

One week after Session 12 Memo (2,500 words + appendices) and the final published Tableau Public dashboard, on ZoneCours; confidential peer ratings
Final examination period Take-home examination — 40%, individual, 3 hours of work in a 48-hour window. Brief and specimen: assessments/take-home/

Prepare for the take-home by re-reading the catch slides from your eleven practice rounds: together they are the course’s list of what each indicator cannot tell a firm.
