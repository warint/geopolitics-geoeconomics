Appendix A — The replications

Each of Sessions 01–11 pairs one big article or book with the data it is built on. Before class you read the work and locate its source. In the 90-minute practice round your team:

  1. Collects the data from the original producer and logs its provenance;
  2. Prepares it in Tableau — pivot, join, recode — recording each decision;
  3. Replicates the target figure as closely as the data allow;
  4. Compares yours with the original, breaks one decision, and extends it in one direction;
  5. Presents one slide — original · ours · the catch — in two minutes, by a member drawn at random.

Every source, its access conditions and its traps: DATA-SOURCES.md.

A.2 Why replicate figures, not regressions

This is not an econometrics course. The figure at the heart of a big work is where its argument meets the reader — the image that ends up in board presentations and ministerial speeches. Rebuilding it forces every decision the authors made into the open: which countries, which years, which deflator, which coding rule, which normalisation. A multinational’s risk team makes the same decisions every time it builds a dashboard. Learning to see them is the point.

A.3 Citing

Cite the work and the dataset, with version and access date. They are distinct contributions, often by different people, and the dataset has usually been revised since the work was published.
